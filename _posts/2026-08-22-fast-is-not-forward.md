*Perceived productivity vs actual productivity in the age of AI agents*
{: .page__lead }

I run a company that builds AI agents. Treat this as a confession.

Everyone I talk to feels faster now. Founders, engineers, salespeople, whole teams. Tabs open, agents running, drafts appearing while you make coffee. The feeling is real. Six months ago a task took a day. Now you fire it at an agent and it comes back in ninety seconds looking done.

Feeling fast and being fast came apart over the last year. Perceived productivity went vertical. Actual productivity moved a lot less than it feels like it did.

"AI is overhyped" is the wrong diagnosis. The speed is not fake. It is just aimed at the wrong thing more often than we admit.

### Volume is not value

An agent that hands you ten options has not made a decision. It has handed you ten decisions. Generating is free now. Choosing is not. The scarce resource was never the draft. It was the judgment about which draft is right, and no amount of generation buys you that. You end the day with forty artifacts and the same three hard calls you started with, except now you are more tired.

### The bottleneck moved. It did not leave

This is the one people miss. When production gets cheap, the constraint relocates to whatever comes next, and what comes next is usually verification. The agent writes the code in a minute. Now you have to read code you did not write, understand intent you did not form, and trust output whose reasoning you cannot see. For a lot of work, reviewing is slower and more expensive than writing. You feel a burst of speed at the moment of creation, then pay it back with interest at the moment of checking. Most people book the first feeling and never subtract the second.

### Cheap production makes us produce things that should not exist

When a strategy doc costs an afternoon, you write it carefully or not at all. When it costs a prompt, everyone has one. Now there are nine strategy docs, a competitive teardown nobody asked for, and a Notion page graveyard. We mistook the cost of making things for the reason we were not making them. Sometimes the friction was the filter.

### Perceived productivity books instantly. Actual productivity clears later

The feeling arrives now. The bill arrives on a delay. The agent output that looked right ships, and three weeks later it is a bug, a wrong number in a board deck, a customer who got a confident answer that happened to be false. Real work has a settlement period. The gap between "looks done" and "is done" is where the illusion lives.

### When the speed is actually real

Agents are absurdly good in one situation: the bottleneck was production, and verification is cheap or automatable. Boilerplate with tests. First drafts you were going to heavily rewrite anyway. Research you can spot-check in a minute. Anything where a machine can also tell you whether the machine was right. Point them there and the speed is not a mirage. It compounds.

The failure mode is aiming them at judgment, at taste, at the one decision the whole quarter turns on, and feeling productive because the screen filled up with text.

Did the actual constraint move? Not "did I produce more." Not "did it feel fast." Did the thing that was actually holding the business back get smaller. If your constraint is a hard product call and you generated forty pages around it, your constraint is exactly where you left it. You just LARPed around it faster.

I am not telling you to use agents less. I sell them. I am telling you to point them at the constraint and count outcomes, not motion. Motion at the speed of light is still just motion.

The speed you feel is not the speed you ship. Aim accordingly.
